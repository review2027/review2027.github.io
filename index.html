Call for Papers

Call for Papers

Peer review is central to the research ecosystem, yet the reviewing process is facing increasing challenges. The growth of research communities, program committees and reviewing workloads has placed considerable pressure on existing reviewing practices. At the same time, concerns about bias, confidentiality, anonymity, conflicts of interest, collusion and the integrity of the decision-making process have become increasingly prominent.

Communities are now experimenting with open and transparent reviewing, stronger forms of reviewer anonymity, tools for detecting collusion and manipulation, and AI-assisted reviewing. Each promises improvements, and each raises new security and privacy questions. What should a trustworthy reviewing system look like? Which parts of today's process should be kept, changed or replaced? Which security and privacy properties should future systems provide, and how should they be balanced against transparency, accountability and meaningful discussion between reviewers?

RE:VIEW will provide a forum for researchers to discuss the future of peer review, with particular emphasis on its security, privacy, integrity and trustworthiness. We encourage submissions that challenge existing assumptions about peer review or propose new models, mechanisms or research directions.

Topics of interest

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Security and privacy requirements for peer-review systems
  • Anonymity, double-blind and triple-blind review
  • Bias, fairness and diversity in reviewing
  • Collusion rings, review manipulation and gaming of review systems
  • Confidentiality and integrity of reviews
  • Transparency, accountability and open peer review
  • Conflicts of interest and reviewer identity
  • Verifiable and auditable peer-review processes
  • Cryptographic approaches to secure and privacy-preserving reviewing
  • Formal security definitions and models for reviewing systems
  • Usability and human factors in secure reviewing
  • AI-assisted reviewing and its security, privacy and integrity implications
  • Reviewer workload, incentives and sustainable reviewing
  • Alternative models of reviewer selection and paper selection
  • The role of codes of conduct and community governance
  • Lessons from existing and emerging reviewing platforms
  • Empirical studies of reviewers, authors, chairs and program committees
  • Proposals for fundamentally new models of peer review

Submissions

RE:VIEW uses a light-touch review process, assessing submissions primarily on their relevance to the scope of the workshop, their originality and potential to stimulate discussion, and their suitability for the workshop's interactive format. This is not a full conference-style technical review. We welcome position papers, empirical findings, new system proposals and provocative ideas that can stimulate discussion about the future of peer review.

All submissions must follow the guidelines below.

Format
All submissions must be in PDF format.
Page limits
At most 5 pages, excluding references.
Anonymity
Reviewing is single-blind: submissions should include author names and affiliations. Given the workshop's light-touch, discussion-oriented review process, formal double-blind anonymisation is not required.
Originality
Submissions must not substantially overlap with papers that have been published (other than as preprints) or accepted for publication, or that are simultaneously submitted to a journal, conference, or workshop with published proceedings.
Conflicts of interest
As defined in the ACNS 2027 Call for Papers: a shared institutional affiliation at the time of submission (including secondary affiliations and consulting), an advisor–advisee relationship at any time, collaboration or joint publication within the prior two years, sponsorship or administration of a grant funding the research, or close personal or family ties. If either program chair has a conflict of interest with a submission, the other chair will handle that submission independently.
Submission site
